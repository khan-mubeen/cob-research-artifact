# v5 evaluation

All 18 cells: six models (gemma, granite, ministral, phi, qwen, smollm3) x three modes (sealed-bid,
reverse auction, supervisor). The charts and the metric tables are generated by
`src/experiments/v5/render_report.py` from the authoritative result files, and every latency and token
value from the per-request records in `evaluation/latency_records/`; no number is typed in.

## Epoch selection and test reads

Each model is fine-tuned once per mode for three epochs; adapters are saved at each epoch boundary.

1. Validation: base and epochs 1, 2 and 3 are evaluated on the full validation split.
2. Selection, per model and mode, on validation only, with a pre-registered ordered key (higher is
   better, an exact tie goes to the lower epoch):
   - sealed-bid: macro decision agreement, then macro JSON validity, then lower macro mean |offer deviation|
   - reverse auction: macro decision accuracy, then mean floor-case accuracy, then offer exact-match rate
   - supervisor: selection accuracy, then JSON validity, then lower mean regret
3. Test, read once each: the base arm (`test_base.json`) and the selected epoch (`test_selected.json`), each
   behind its own one-shot gate (`authorize_base_test`, `authorize_test`), which refuses a second read.
4. The other epochs are then read on the same test rows as descriptive results
   (`test_descriptive_epoch<N>.json`), tagged "descriptive, not used for selection". Selection and
   compare_epochs refuse them as input, and the descriptive path never touches the one-shot gates.

The rule-based decision function produced the labels, so it is the ground truth: a reference line at
100 % agreement and 0 deviation, not a competitor bar. For sealed-bid and reverse auction most rows are
BID, so the always-BID baseline is drawn on the decision-agreement charts. Price and days deviations
for sealed-bid and reverse auction are absolute, never signed.

For the supervisor, the ground truth is `decide_chain_selection` in `src/common/bidding_formulas.py`. All
eligible stage combinations are considered, chains over the budget or past the deadline are discarded, and
the rest are ranked by the order's priority: urgent by fewest total days, then lowest total cost; flexible
by lowest total cost, then fewest total days; normal by the lowest equal-weighted score of cost / budget,
days / days remaining and compound risk / maximum defect rate, with ties broken by cost and then days.
Any remaining tie goes to the lowest agent ids. The model receives a bounded candidate list that includes
the reference winner; it does not enumerate every combination itself. These rules label the supervisor
data and score the learned supervisor; "the optimal plan" below means the chain they select.

## Metric definitions

| metric | definition | better |
|---|---|---|
| Decision agreement (sealed-bid, reverse auction) | The share of rows whose BID or NO_BID decision matches the rule-based decision, per stage, then the mean over the three stages, in percent. | higher |
| Price deviation (sealed-bid, reverse auction) | `abs(model_offer - label_offer) / label_offer * 100` per row, the mean per stage, then the mean over the three stages, in percent. | lower |
| Completion-time deviation | Sealed-bid and reverse auction: `abs(predicted_days - expected_days)` per row, the mean per stage, then the mean over the three stages, in days. Supervisor: the mean of the absolute per-row percentage deviation of the chosen plan from the optimal plan, in percent. | lower |
| Selection accuracy (supervisor) | The share of decisions in which the model picks the optimal plan, in percent. | higher |
| Cost deviation (supervisor) | The mean of the absolute per-row percentage deviation of the chosen plan's cost from the optimal plan, in percent. | lower |
| Latency | The time of the `generate` call for one decision, warm-up rows excluded. Milliseconds in the tables, seconds in the charts. | lower |
| TTFT | The time to the first generated token. | lower |
| TPOT | The time per output token after the first: (latency - TTFT) / (output tokens - 1). | lower |
| Output tokens | The generated tokens per decision, counted from the generated token ids, including the stop token, at most 200. | lower |

"Macro decision accuracy" in the reverse-auction selection key (see "Epoch selection and test reads") is the
same quantity as "Decision agreement" in the tables and charts. The charts plot the value from the selection
record itself.

## Reading the charts

The charts carry no title and no footnote. Their notes (the selected-epoch ring, one-shot and descriptive
test reads, the zoom strip, the McNemar flags, the completion-time precision, units, the Always-BID line,
the latency conditions and the radar scoring) are in `captions.md`.

- **Macro averages.** Sealed-bid and reverse-auction values are macro averages over the three stages
  (material, tailoring, logistics): "macro decision agreement over the three stages"
  (`src/experiments/v5/epoch_selection.py`), computed as
  `_mean(summary[s]["decision_agreement_pct"] for s in STAGES)` for the decision metric and
  `macro = {k: _mean([per[st][k] for st in STAGES]) for k in overall if k != "n"}`
  (`render_report.py`) for the deviations. Supervisor values are means over all supervisor rows, not
  macro averages.
- **Absolute deviation.** The size of the gap from the rule-based value, whatever its direction. Price:
  `abs(model_offer - label_offer) / label_offer * 100`, in percent. Completion time: `abs(predicted_days - expected_days)`,
  in days. Supervisor cost and completion time: the mean of the absolute per-row percentage deviation of
  the chosen plan from the optimal plan.
- **Zoom strip.** The strip beside each chart magnifies the shaded band on the main plot, at the band's
  exact width; validation and test of a metric share both ranges. For decision agreement the band is
  0.15 percentage points wide for sealed-bid (99.85 to 100.00) and 0.6 for reverse auction (99.4 to
  100.0); for supervisor selection accuracy it runs from 92 to 100.
- **Ring and McNemar flags.** The ring, labelled "Selected on validation", marks the epoch selected on
  validation only. Selections whose epoch is not significantly different from the runner-up (exact
  McNemar p >= 0.05) are named in `captions.md` and in the table at the end of this file; the charts no
  longer mark them.
- **Test charts.** Base and the epoch selected on validation are the one-shot test reads; the other
  epochs on a test chart are descriptive only and were never used for selection.
- **Completion-time precision.** The sealed-bid and reverse-auction completion-time values carry 4
  decimals, so the epoch differences inside those zoom strips are at the precision limit of the stored
  values.
- **Latency.** Whole test split, batch size 1, one GCD, warm-up rows excluded. Each decision is timed
  alone, one request at a time, and each measuring process ran on one GCD of an AMD MI250X. The warm-up rows (the first 3 rows of each shard and the first row after a resume) are
  excluded from the latency statistics; token statistics use every row. The fine-tuned bar is the epoch selected on validation ("Fine-tuned (model colour)" in the
  legend). The fine-tuned arm was timed with its LoRA adapter attached, not merged into the base
  weights: the adapter directory is loaded with Unsloth's `FastLanguageModel.from_pretrained` and no
  merge is called. Generation is greedy with a 200-token cap, and output tokens include the stop token.
  Each request also records its time to first token (TTFT) through a stopping-criterion hook that never
  stops generation; time per output token is TPOT = (total - TTFT) / (output tokens - 1). The latency
  charts, the radar speed axis and `tables/latency_breakdown_*` all read these per-request timings,
  published in `latency_records/`.
- **Radar charts.** Six fine-tuned models per mode, each at its epoch selected on validation, are scored on
  five axes. Farther out is better on every axis. Each score runs from 0 to 10: 0 is the weakest value
  among the twelve base and fine-tuned models of the mode, rounded to a clean number on the weak side, and
  10 is the best possible value (100 % accuracy, or zero deviation, latency or output tokens). Price or cost
  accuracy, time accuracy, speed and token efficiency are scored from the price or cost deviation, the
  completion-time deviation, the latency and the output tokens per decision, and decision agreement or
  selection accuracy from its own value. Each chart draws three of the six fine-tuned models: the top three
  by the mean of the three validation scores (accuracy, price or cost accuracy, time accuracy), with ties
  broken by the validation accuracy score and then by alphabetical order. Speed and token efficiency are not
  part of the ranking. The drawn models are shown with their test scores on all five axes; latency and
  output tokens were measured on the test split only. Each model is one colour, drawn as a thin line without
  markers; the polygon with the largest area is drawn first, so the smaller ones lie on top. All values,
  references, scores and ranks, with the score formulas, are in `tables/radar_values.md`, including those of
  the three models that are not drawn. The margin to the fourth model is no more than 0.002 score points.
  Latency for all six models is in the latency chart.
- **Token tables.** `tables/tokens_summary.{md,tex}` and `tables/tokens.{md,csv,tex}` give values per
  decision on the whole test split, for the base model and the epoch selected on validation, computed from
  `latency_records/`.

## How to read the latency tables

`tables/latency_breakdown_*` and `latency_records/` cover all 36 arms (base and the epoch selected on
validation of the 18 mode and model cells) on the whole test split: 67,597 rows per sealed-bid arm, 75,397
per reverse-auction arm and 4,435 per supervisor arm. Each decision ran alone (batch size 1) with greedy
decoding and at most 200 new tokens; output tokens include the stop token.

- **Latency** (L) is the time of the `generate` call for one decision. **TTFT** (F) is the time to the
  first generated token. **TPOT** is the time per output token after the first, (L - F) / (n - 1) for a
  request with n output tokens.
- **Warm-up rows** are the first three rows of every shard and the first row after a resume. Latency
  statistics leave them out; `tokens.*` and the arm csv use every row for tokens. Table 3 gives the
  warm-up mean, its ratio to the steady mean and the number of warm-up rows per arm.
- Tables 2 and 4 use the rows that are steady in both arms of a cell; Table 1 and `tokens.*` use every row.
- **Decomposition** (Table 4). With n the mean output tokens, F the mean TTFT, L the mean latency and
  T = (L - F) / (n - 1):
  L_ft - L_base = (F_ft - F_base) + (n_ft - n_base) x T_base + (n_ft - 1) x (T_ft - T_base),
  the TTFT part, the output length effect and the per token effect. It is an accounting identity, not a
  cause.
- 103 steady requests in 11 arms had a first token only after more than 1 s (up to 275.7 s). They widen the
  standard deviation of those arms (reverse-auction Granite base, for example, from 114 ms without them to
  1,612 ms) and raise the arm means by at most 0.66 %.
- TPOT in Table 2 is the mean of the per-request values, while Table 4 uses ratios of means, so
  recomputing Table 4 from Table 2 agrees only up to rounding.
- Mean latency per arm differs by -5.8 % to +6.49 % between the original profile and the new run, each
  mean taken over the steady rows of the whole test split in that run
  (`tables/latency_breakdown_vs_original_profile.md`).
- `src/experiments/v5/check_latency_tables.py` recomputes the relations between the latency tables from the
  published csv and markdown files and exits non-zero on any mismatch.
- `tables/adapter_merge_diagnostic_*` is a separate timing control only: on 300 validation rows per arm it
  times the base model, the adapter attached and the adapter merged into the weights. The merged model is
  not output-identical to the attached one, so it compares timing, not outputs. The tooling for both
  measurements is in `src/experiments/v5/latency_measurement/`.

## Original profile and latency records

`reports/v5/results/latency/` contains the `meta.json` summaries for all arms and the supervisor `.jsonl`
records. The original sealed-bid and reverse-auction `.jsonl` files are omitted because each exceeds 100 MB.
The latency charts, the radar speed axis and the latency and token statistics use the included
`latency_records/`, which also records time to first token. `tables/latency_breakdown_vs_original_profile.{md,csv}` shows both for all 36 arms.
Output-token counts agree on every row in 23 arms; Phi sealed-bid base differs on 167 rows (0.25 %) and
Gemma reverse-auction base on 2 rows. In the SmolLM3 and Qwen arms up to 21.78 % of rows differ (SmolLM3
sealed-bid base), where mean output tokens move from 146.89 to 148.85, the largest change. Mean latency
per arm differs by -5.8 % to +6.49 %. The cause of the output differences is not identified.

## The qwen reverse_auction stop-token fix

The qwen reverse_auction adapters end every answer with `<|im_end|>` (id 248046), the token the chat
template writes after an assistant turn and the one in their training labels. The checkpoint's
generation config stops only on `<|endoftext|>` (248044), so generation ran past the answer, began a new
turn and repeated the JSON up to the 200-token cap; the parser (first `{` to last `}`) then rejected the
doubled object. The first evaluation scored the cell near 0 % (an output-format artifact; the first JSON
object gave the correct decision on every validation row).

Fix, no retraining (the end token is in the training labels and no row is truncated): evaluation
generation also stops on the chat template's end-of-turn id when the model does not already stop on it
(`src/experiments/v5/stop_rule.py`). Checked on validation for all 18 cells: the five other models
already stop on their end-of-turn token, so the rule changes nothing for them; for the other qwen cells it
changes outputs only by a trailing newline, so it is applied to qwen reverse_auction only. A 201-row
validation probe passed (0 % run-on, 100 % strict JSON, 100 % decision agreement for epochs 1-3). The
cell's old results were deleted (`provenance/deleted_qwen_ra.txt`) and it was re-evaluated from scratch:
validation, selection with the same rule, and one deliberately re-authorized test read for base and the
selected epoch.

## Disclosures and how to read the records

- The qwen reverse_auction correction above re-read the test split: its base and selected-epoch test
  reads were deliberately re-authorized after the first results were deleted. The descriptive test arms
  (non-selected epochs) were read after selection and are reported, never used for selection.
- Historical hashes: the authorization blocks in the test results and the per-result provenance records
  keep the sha256 values of the files as they were before the restructure, while their paths point at
  the new locations. Resolve them through `provenance/old_to_new_paths.csv`, which maps each old path to
  its new one with both hashes.
- The generation stop rule is applied by the canonical run name `<model>-<mode>-v5-r01`. A custom, smoke
  or `r02` run name is not recognised, so its model must be given explicitly (`--model-name` in
  `evaluate.py` and `evaluate_sharded.py`); otherwise qwen reverse_auction would fall outside the rule.
- The original profiling records (`results/latency/`) carry `git_commit: "unknown"` by design: they were
  produced with `COB_NO_GIT=1`, so no git process ran (`latency_records/` has no commit field).
  The recorded source fingerprints identify the evaluation code: `provenance/code_sha256_at_campaign_start.txt`
  holds the hashes at the start of the campaign, and `provenance/code_sha256.csv` the hashes before and after
  the changes made during it. Four listed files changed later, and 13 listed files are not in this repository
  (see "Code changes after the freeze"). The `transformers`, `peft` and `unsloth` packages of the
  Python environment were installed before the first evaluation and the first profiling record, and none of
  their files changed afterwards.
- Three documents moved from the former `reports/v5/_migration/` folder to `evaluation/provenance/`:
  `code_sha256.csv` (evaluation code hashes before and after the change), `old_to_new_paths.csv` (the
  restructure ledger) and `deleted_qwen_ra.txt` (the deleted qwen reverse_auction results and the
  re-authorization). Their hashes, ids, dates and facts are unchanged; host paths are written relative to the
  repository root, `<scratch>/` stands for the project scratch copy of the repository, and the former report
  folder and its report script are named `old_report`. `provenance.csv` writes its paths the same way.
  The `code_identity` field in `results/records/**/records.*` still names the old location of `code_sha256.csv`.

## Regenerate

Charts need no GPU. Use a separate environment with `requirements-evaluation.txt`; matching the recorded
Python 3.12 rendering environment is needed for pixel comparisons. The bundled Source Sans 3 fonts are
under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (`src/experiments/v5/fonts/LICENSE.md`).

To rebuild the main figures and tables from the included files, without overwriting published records:

    python -m src.experiments.v5.render_report --skip-original-profile --out build/evaluation --csv-out build/evaluation/tables
    python src/experiments/v5/check_latency_tables.py --tables build/evaluation/tables

Use a fresh output directory: `--csv-out` refuses to replace existing CSVs. The new flag skips only
`latency_breakdown_vs_original_profile.{md,csv}`, not the latency tables or charts. All calculations and
selection rules are unchanged. This is a report rebuild from saved results, not a new model evaluation.
See [setup and reproducibility](../../../docs/reproducibility.md) for environment details.

The historical full rebuild, without that flag, requires the original sealed-bid and reverse-auction profiling `.jsonl` files, which
are not included. That workflow reads these first and stops before rendering charts if they are absent.
Use the explicit flag above for a rebuild from the public files; there is no silent fallback.

With the omitted records restored and the packages in `requirements-evaluation.txt` installed, run
from the repository root (Bash syntax):

    PYTHONPATH=. python -m src.experiments.v5.render_report

This rewrites the 18 metric charts, the three latency charts and the three radar charts, `tables/metrics.md`,
`tables/completion_time_deviation.md`, `tables/radar_values.md`, `tables/tokens_summary.{md,tex}` and
`tables/tokens.{md,tex}` (both from `tables/tokens.csv`), and `tables/latency_breakdown_*` (through
`src/experiments/v5/latency_measurement/summarize.py`). Inputs: `reports/v5/selection/` (results),
`reports/v5/results/records/` (provenance), `latency_records/`, `tables/tokens.csv`,
`reports/v5/results/latency/` (original profiling records, including the omitted files needed for the
comparison table), `tables/latency_output_cap.csv`
(the output cap of each latency arm),
`models/training/` (training status in `provenance.csv` only; git-ignored, and without it the rows read
"training state absent"), `tables/always_bid_baseline.csv` (the always-BID line of the validation and test
decision-agreement charts, read first) and `data/finetune/v5/` (not in this repository; read only for a split
the table lacks, and a split in neither gets no line). `tokens.csv`,
`completion_time_deviation.csv` and `provenance.csv` are never overwritten: `--csv-out <empty folder>`
writes fresh copies there, and `--out <empty folder> --csv-out <empty folder>/tables` rebuilds every
published file into an empty folder, subject to the same input requirements.

To rebuild only the latency tables, including the original-profile comparison (also requires the
omitted records):

    PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python src/experiments/v5/latency_measurement/summarize.py \
        --publish reports/v5/evaluation/latency_records --out <folder>

To check consistency of the published latency tables without those records or additional packages:

    python src/experiments/v5/check_latency_tables.py
